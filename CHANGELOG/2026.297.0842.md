# 2026.297.0842 — docs(rulebook): say what a merge commit owes the attribution trailer

## Summary

The umbrella's *AI Attribution* section says every AI-authored commit ends with the
`Assisted-by:` trailer. It did not say what a **merge commit** is — and a merge commit
is exactly the case where two readings are defensible: it brings upstream's commits in
and adds no content of its own, while its own message is the agent's authorship. The
rulebook picked neither, so a published merge commit carrying no trailer was neither
clearly a violation nor clearly compliant, and the org could not tell which. Three such
commits sit on one published branch, one per review round's catch-up merge
(opencharly/opencharly#613).

**This PR states the rule, once:** a merge commit is judged by its **message**, not by
its content. A merge the agent writes ends with the trailer like any other commit; a
merge whose message the agent did **not** write — the one `gh pr update-branch` writes
on GitHub's behalf — carries no trailer and owes none.

| file | what it carries |
|---|---|
| `AGENTS.md` | the *AI Attribution* section's new paragraph — what a merge commit owes the `Assisted-by:` trailer, and the one exemption |

The rule matches what the org already does: a published catch-up merge in the umbrella's
own history carries the trailer (`95e91f5`), and the three that do not are exactly the
ones the finding names.

**The other half of the finding is the control**, and it lands in the repo that owns the
self-audit: `opencharly/layer-charly-internals` gains a preflight arm that reads every
commit in the pushed range and resolves the ones that owe a trailer. That leg follows
this one, and it lands before the corpus and the umbrella's pins move.

Refs opencharly/opencharly#613
