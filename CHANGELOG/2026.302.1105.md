The umbrella — the org's own view of itself — still named the commits from before that cutover, so its three pins carried the retired selector one step further than the three repos that had already fixed it.

**The RCA.**

- **Mechanism** — the umbrella records every org repo as a gitlink; the pin is the INDEX entry and the checkout follows it. Nothing advanced the umbrella's three pins after the producer legs landed, so the umbrella's view of those three repos stayed on the pre-cutover commits while the repos themselves had moved on.
- **Missed control** — the currency audit (`charly task pins --param MODE=currency`) REPORTS a behind pin and does not fail, because "behind" is an advance available NOW rather than a violation: the remedy is a pin-wave PR, not a gate. So nothing turned red, and the three pins stayed behind until a wave ran.
- **Blast radius** — every consumer of the umbrella's view — the harness skill bindings in `.dsh/skills` (symlinks into the corpus checkout), the org map, and every repo that reads the umbrella as its source of pins — read the pre-cutover bodies.
- **Root fix** — advance the leaf pins in the index with the sanctioned wave (`charly task sync`), materialize the checkouts at the new pins (`charly task init`), and judge the committed tree (`charly task verify`). No hand-pin, no branch checkout.

**The wave's order.** `charly task sync` (the pins move in the index; the checkouts that exist are rolled) → `charly task init` (those checkouts are refreshed to the pins the index records) → `charly task verify` (every pin judged from the index, on the committed tree). The `sync` verb holds this worktree's index for the minutes it rolls the submodules, so no other git command ran in this worktree while it ran.

**The wave's scope was exactly these three leaves.** The verb's own report is the scope control: it rolled 7 leaf submodules to their default-branch HEAD and advanced 396 leaf pins in the index, and the 6 `pin_map` edges moved 0, because the edge half follows charly's own `box/*` gitlinks and the `charly` leaf is already current. The first wave moved 2 pins (one rolled in its checkout, one advanced in the index) and the completing wave moved 1 (advanced in the index, because `docs` carries no checkout) — three in all, and nothing else.

**Why the wave ran twice, stated rather than hidden.** The scope is derived from the currency report, and the report is a snapshot of the remotes at the moment it runs. The first wave ran at `2026-10-29T10:24:30Z`–`10:28:38Z` and moved two pins. The site's producer leg (opencharly/docs#205) merged at `2026-10-29T10:30:07Z` — about 90 seconds AFTER the first wave ended, and about 12 seconds before the commit that would have carried it was written. At wave time `docs`'s default branch tip WAS `84595508` — the umbrella's recorded pin — so `docs` was correctly `cur` and correctly not advanced. A completing wave then moved that third pin. This is the sanctioned remedy, not a hand-pin (R3): the pin contract's unit is the wave, and the wave advances whatever the report names at the moment it runs.

**A caveat this body states rather than hides.** For each producer, the newest tag points at the MERGE commit, and the served head the umbrella now pins is exactly ONE commit above that tag — and that one commit adds only `CHANGELOG/<version>.md`.
