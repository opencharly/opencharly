**The `!` subject marker was undefined, and nothing related it to the body's "Breaking change" line.** The marker was written down in exactly ONE line of the org's rulebook — the hard-cutover checklist in `cutover-policy` — and neither `AGENTS.md` nor the `git-workflow` skill's body spec said what it meant. So a merged changelog could carry a bang-marked subject (*this change is a hard cutover*) beside a body that said nothing about what breaks for this repo's consumers, and no gate noticed. Measured org-wide: of the **53 bang-marked merged pull requests** in the scanned window (the 300 most recently updated merged pull requests org-wide), **51 carry no breaking-change answer at all**; the two that do (opencharly/opencharly#801 and opencharly/plugin-box#66) both answer with a REASONED `none`, and **not one of the 53 carries a bare `none`**. The issue is opencharly/opencharly#802.

**The rule — one relation, three sides, each stated by the surface that owns it.**

- The `!` marker means **this change carries a HARD CUTOVER** — the whole phase lands in one change, with no shim, no dual path and no follow-up (`/charly-internals:cutover-policy`).
- The body's `## Change classification` answers **what breaks for THIS repo's consumers, and how to roll it back** (`/charly-internals:git-workflow`).
- So under a `!` the body MUST carry a `**Breaking change + rollback:**` line, and **a bare `none` is not an answer** — a cutover that breaks nothing HERE must say WHY it breaks nothing here (the shape opencharly/opencharly#801's changelog already has: *"none — the pointers move to commits the org has already merged"*).

Without a `!` the line stays optional, and a body that declares a breakage without the marker is judged on its own merits.

**The control — arm M in `scripts/check-pr-body-diff.mjs`.** The head's SUBJECT and the body's `## Change classification` are read together, and a bang-marked head whose body carries no answer, or a bare `none`, is a finding that names the cause. The subject is the marker source because it is what the PR title carries: measured over the umbrella's last 24 merged pull requests, the title and the merged commit's subject agree on the MARKER in every one of them — although all 24 differ in wording, the merged subject carrying the pull request's number or the tag-on-merge rewording.

**The mirror direction was measured away and is NOT implemented.** A body that declares a breakage does NOT imply a `!`: opencharly/marketplace#602's merged body declares a breaking change while its title carries no marker, so that direction would fire on a legitimate merge. Only the `!` ⇒ answer-required direction is mechanized.

**The bare-`none` half of the arm is prophylactic, and says so.** No pull request in the org produces that shape today (0 of the 53 measured), so that half of the arm forbids a form the org has not yet written. That is what a rule is for: it has to hold before the first instance, not after it.

**What this change deliberately does NOT touch.** The org's validator rulebook (`action-review/charly.yml` and the live org variable) is NOT edited: that is a security-machinery change, and it would need a maintainer sign-off an agent may not post on its own initiative. The relation is checked by the umbrella's OWN gate, which reads this repo's own tree and this repo's own body — no validator spec, no org variable, no T4. The org-wide half — the corpus's own `pr_body_lint.py` arm — is **FILED** as opencharly/marketplace#603, because a linter must not enforce an obligation no agent could have read: the rule has to be published (here, then projected into the corpus) before the linter arms it.
