The reason is a real coupling, not hygiene: the older SDK's `vmshared/spec_aliases.go` re-exports `spec` types the newer `spec` dropped, and the SDK helper package that imports those aliases fails to build — so a `spec`-only bump in a consumer does not compile. Any future consumer of `spec`'s generation surface moves its `spec` and `sdk` requires in the same commit.

### The no-checkout trap: what the currency report gets right, and the one field it gets wrong

A fresh session worktree carries a derived working set only, and a pin with no checkout cannot have its ancestry judged. The first reading of this wave printed, for both advancing pins, `differs (no checkout to judge ancestry)`; the second, with the two checkouts materialized, printed `behind` and named both legs. **The label was right in the first reading, the recorded and served commits were right, and the `2 of 409` count was right** — an unjudged pin is reported, never folded into a verdict.

What the first reading got wrong is its last field. With nothing but unjudged rows it printed `producer-first legs: none reachable from this repo`, and the pin contract defines that phrase as *"this repo HAS stale pins, but every one of them is blocked by a producer it cannot reach"* — while instructing the reader to *"read the legs as the work list, not as decoration"*. Nothing was blocked in this tree: both remotes were read successfully and both advances were available the moment their checkouts existed. A reader who takes the legs as the work list concludes "waiting on another repo" and skips the wave.

That is a defect in the pin verb's report, not in this repo's pins, and it is filed rather than fixed here (R1's file-it path): **opencharly/plugin-task#64** carries the mechanism, both readings, the blast radius and the fix direction. This body states it instead of dismissing it, which is what round 1 asked for.
